\begin{abstract}
Intervention-based probe rankings can mistake damage to a downstream decoder for evidence that a candidate probe found a causally important direction. We audit a multi-model NLP pipeline in which the decoder both supplies an edit and scores it, while the reported target is constant across candidates. In a registered matched-versus-split control over \SplitCells{} model--task cells, AlterRep target damage falls from \SplitMatched{} under the generating decoder to \SplitIndependent{} under a separately trained evaluator (gap \SplitGap{}, 95\% cell-bootstrap interval \SplitCI{}; exact sign-flip $p=\SplitP$). After excluding all records affected by three implementation bugs, \BugFilteredN{} records remain: the cell score has median \BugFilteredMedian{}, \BugFilteredCeiling{} are exactly $1.0$, and AlterRep is the first maximizer in \BugFilteredAlterRep{}. We derive a five-part scored-object validity protocol and run prespecified perturbation-budget, orientation, and fresh-decoder checks. Fixed-decoder damage alone is not evidence of concept erasure or probe reliability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A probe-ranking target is useful only if it measures the candidate being ranked. In causal probing, a score can instead describe the edit, evaluator, or representation. We trace those dependencies before interpreting correlations with probe properties.

Our central control isolates one such failure mode. AlterRep uses a decoder-derived direction to edit a representation. When the same decoder also scores that edit, mean target damage is \SplitMatched{}. Replacing it with a separately trained split evaluator reduces mean damage to \SplitIndependent{}, a gap of \SplitGap{} across \SplitCells{} model--task cells. The 95\% cell-bootstrap interval is \SplitCI{}, and an exact two-sided sign-flip test gives $p=\SplitP$. The edit is unchanged; only the scoring decoder changes.

Three bugs affected model states, labels, or candidate duplication: a padding error collapsed Gemma states, WinoGender preprocessing removed the disambiguating pronoun, and a no-gradient kernel duplicated two MLP variants. Removing every affected Phase-1 row leaves the abstract's bug-filtered result unchanged. The benchmark is an internal, unpublished adaptation of \citet{canby2025reliable}; unlike their intervention-level framework, it reuses fixed probes, maximizes across methods, and copies one cell score to every candidate. We audit only this adaptation.

Linear probes show decodability, not model use \citep{alain2017understanding,hewitt2019designing,belinkov2022probing}. Interventions such as INLP, RLACE, amnesic probing, and LEACE add evidence under method-specific assumptions \citep{ravfogel2020null,ravfogel2022linear,elazar2021amnesic,belrose2023leace}; probe-derived directions can also mix concept and non-concept features \citep{kumar2022unreliable}. Our contribution is the falsifiable protocol in Table~\ref{tab:protocol}: trace candidate identity, separate editing from evaluation, test inversion and redecoding, separate examples, and cluster inference at independent units.

\section{Evaluator reuse changes measured damage}
\label{sec:split}

The matched-versus-split control holds the edit fixed and changes only the evaluator. For each of six models, we use the middle layer on SVA and SST-2. Five independently seeded attacker/evaluator pairs train on sentence-disjoint examples. The attacker generates each edit. The edited representations are then scored either by that attacker (matched) or by its paired evaluator (split). We average the five pair-level contrasts within each model--task cell and conduct inference over the \SplitCells{} cell means.

AlterRep's mean target damage drops from \SplitMatched{} to \SplitIndependent{}. The mean paired gap is \SplitGap{}, with a 95\% cell-bootstrap interval of \SplitCI{} and exact two-sided sign-flip $p=\SplitP$. All seven nonzero cell gaps are positive; the other five are zero. The effect varies across model--task cells. Because the edited representations are held fixed, the gap identifies evaluator reuse as a source of measured damage in this pipeline.

\section{The audited target cannot rank candidates}
\label{sec:audited-target}

\paragraph{Benchmark and provenance.}
The internal benchmark crosses six transformer families, SVA \citep{linzen2016assessing}, SST-2 \citep{socher2013recursive}, and WinoGender \citep{rudinger2018gender}, five layers, three probe architectures, and 20 seeds. Models are BERT, GPT-2, Pythia, Qwen, Gemma, and Llama \citep{devlin2019bert,radford2019language,biderman2023pythia,qwen2024qwen25,gemmateam2024gemma2,grattafiori2024llama}. A preregistered Hessian association failed; we then registered the computation-graph audit, matched-versus-split control, and candidate-conditioned diagnostic. Appendix~\ref{app:design} gives folds, gates, exclusions, and inference units.

\paragraph{The audited score.}
Phase 1 trains fixed linear target and control evaluators for each cell. Let $a^{Z_c}_{\mathrm{pre}}$ and $a^{Z_c}_{\mathrm{post},m}$ denote target-evaluator accuracy before and after edit $m$; define $a^{Z_e}$ analogously for the control evaluator. The pipeline records
\begin{align}
\widetilde C_m&=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{a^{Z_c}_{\mathrm{pre}}-a^{Z_c}_{\mathrm{post},m}}{a^{Z_c}_{\mathrm{pre}}-0.5},0,1\right), &
\widetilde S_m&=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{a^{Z_e}_{\mathrm{post},m}-0.5}{a^{Z_e}_{\mathrm{pre}}-0.5},0,1\right),
\end{align}
then computes $H(C,S)=2CS/(C+S)$ when $C+S>0$, with $H(0,0)=0$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phase1}
\RPmax(g)=\max_{m\in\{\mathrm{INLP,RLACE,AlterRep,FGSM,PGD}\}}H(\widetilde C_m,\widetilde S_m).
\end{equation}
Neither the accuracy ratios nor the maximum in Eq.~\ref{eq:phase1} comes from \citet{canby2025reliable}. Further, $\widetilde C$ measures damage to one fixed decoder. It does not certify information removal.

\paragraph{Candidate independence.}
The pipeline copies $\RPmax(g)$ to all 60 candidates in cell $g$, so each cell has one target value despite candidate test-accuracy ranges of $0.46$--$0.94$ on SVA and $0.50$--$0.89$ on SST-2. Pooled correlations repeat a cell outcome across architectures and seeds; they cannot identify a within-cell ranking.

\begin{proposition}[Cell-constant targets]
\label{prop:constant}
If $T(c,g)=t_g$ for every $c\in\mathcal C_g$, then $T$ induces a complete tie within each cell. Any pooled Spearman association between a candidate statistic $X$ and $T$ contains only between-cell information.
\end{proposition}

The proof and weighting consequences appear in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.

\paragraph{Aggregation and orientation.}
Across 5,400 records, 48.9\% equal $1.0$ and the median is $0.9999$; replacing the maximum by a mean removes much of this ceiling but is not a validated substitute. The structural failure survives the bug-filtered analysis. The accuracy ratio also confuses inversion with loss: a balanced decoder can move from perfect to zero accuracy while complemented predictions remain perfect. Calibration-frozen orientation metrics and fresh decoders distinguish these cases.

\paragraph{Candidate-conditioned diagnostic.}
$\Dcand$ projects the leading right-singular direction of each candidate's logit-difference Jacobian and scores the edit with five separately trained evaluators. This repairs candidate identity, maximum aggregation, and direct evaluator reuse, but not every validity condition. It recovers planted rankings in linear and held-out nonlinear synthetic families, yet 93.3\% of nonlinear scores lie within $0.02$ of one, above the registered 20\% saturation limit; the locked battery therefore rejects it. The real-data Hessian association ($\rho=-0.042$) remains descriptive.

\section{Scope, responsible use, and conclusion}

The scope is narrow: the benchmark is internal and unpublished, WinoGender is small and flawed, 34 of 90 Phase-2 cells are excluded, model--task data are reused across layers, and no downstream language-model behavior is measured. The candidate diagnostic removes one direction, protects one control, and fails its nonlinear synthetic gate. These facts rule out claims about erasure, prevalence, or Hessian geometry in general.

The original archives also lack Phase-2 per-example predictions, edited representations for fresh-decoder training, failed layer-evaluator accuracies, and Hessian eigenvectors. Orientation and post-edit redecodability are therefore untested in the archived study. We include them in Table~\ref{tab:protocol} because they are necessary falsification checks, not because our pipeline satisfied them.

Invalid scores can support false claims about concept removal, bias mitigation, or safety. Here, the same AlterRep edit appears nearly complete under its generating decoder and substantially weaker under a split evaluator, invalidating the score as a probe-quality target. Similar claims should first trace the scored object, separate editing from evaluation, test inversion and redecodability, and align inference with shared data.

\section{Complete proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:constant}}
\label{app:proof}

Fix a finite set of cells $g$ and candidates $c\in\mathcal C_g$. Assume $T(c,g)=t_g$. For any two candidates $c_1,c_2\in\mathcal C_g$, $T(c_1,g)=T(c_2,g)$, so the target induces a complete within-cell tie. Let $U$ be the pooled midrank of candidate statistic $X$ and $V$ the pooled midrank of $T$. Since $T$ is constant within $g$, $V=v_g$ almost surely conditional on $G=g$. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(U,V\mid G=g)
=\operatorname{Cov}(U,v_g\mid G=g)=0.
\]
The law of total covariance gives
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)
&=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(U,V\mid G)]
+\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbb E[U\mid G],\mathbb E[V\mid G])\\
&=\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbb E[U\mid G],v_G).
\end{align*}
Spearman correlation is Pearson correlation of midranks, so its covariance numerator contains only covariance between cell-average candidate ranks and cell target ranks. Candidate replication can change cell weights, marginal rank scaling, and the nominal number of rows, but it cannot add target information that distinguishes candidates inside a cell. If every cell has the same target, $V$ has zero variance and the pooled Spearman correlation is undefined.

\section{Experimental design and provenance}
\label{app:design}

\paragraph{Phase 1.}
Each model--layer--task cell uses the same extracted representations, target labels, control labels, fixed target/control evaluators, and five interventions for all 60 candidates. The candidate probes differ by architecture and seed. The audited target is the maximum in Eq.~\ref{eq:phase1}. The original prediction used raw mean absolute cosine between flattened probe parameters and the top 20 Hessian eigenvectors. That statistic changes with parameter dimension and with the basis chosen inside repeated eigenspaces, so the failed re-test cannot answer a basis-invariant geometry question.

\paragraph{Matched-versus-split control.}
For each of six models, we use the middle layer for SVA and SST-2. Five seeded attacker/evaluator pairs train on separate examples. The attacker generates each edit. Target and control damage are then scored once by that attacker and once by its paired evaluator. We compute the primary AlterRep contrast on the \SplitCells{} cell means. The reported percentile interval resamples cells with replacement; the exact sign-flip test flips the sign of each nonzero paired cell difference. A second bootstrap that resamples both cells and the five within-cell pairs gives $[0.164,0.529]$.

\paragraph{Candidate diagnostic.}
A four-way sentence-disjoint split assigns 40\% of examples to candidate fitting, 20\% to evaluator fitting, 30\% to direction estimation and scoring, and 10\% to candidate testing. A model--task run must pass middle-layer target/control learnability and shuffled-label gates. Each layer then requires five bagged target/control evaluators above $0.60$ out-of-bag accuracy; denominators must exceed a $0.55$ floor. The run yields 3,360 candidate records from 56 of 90 possible cells. Fifteen cells fail model--task gates and 19 fail layer-evaluator gates. Fourteen of the latter occur at the first two sampled layers. Only Pythia and GPT-2 contribute WinoGender cells.

\paragraph{Synthetic validation.}
The generator draws orthonormal target, control, and shortcut axes in $\mathbb R^{256}$. It varies the probability that shortcut and target labels agree during candidate training, then evaluates with independent target and shortcut labels. Linear ground truth is absolute cosine with the planted target axis. The held-out nonlinear family uses mean principal-angle cosine with a planted two-dimensional XOR subspace. The delivered rows omit one registered correlated target--shortcut condition. The main text reports both pooled and within-replicate/shortcut associations. The registered battery requires all rank, range, dissociation, random-control, and saturation checks; the nonlinear saturation failure fixes the overall outcome.
